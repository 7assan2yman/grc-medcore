Cybersecurity Capstone Project — Choose Your Track
20 Project Options — GRC · Penetration Testing · SOC (Attack & Defend) — Teams of 6

 Team Size                    6 students per team                    Categories               GRC (7) · Pentesting (7) · SOC (6)

 Deliverables                 Written report + presentation + evidence (screenshots, logs, diagrams)



  Before You Start — Rules for Everyone

  1. Pick your team of 6 and assign every role listed under your chosen project — nobody sits out, and each of you will be graded
  individually on your role.
  2. All hacking, scanning, and attack/defense work happens only inside the lab environment your instructor gives you. Never point any
  tool at a real, external target.
  3. Keep evidence as you go — screenshots, logs, notes. If you don't document it, it didn't happen.
  4. Your final report must include: what you did, how you did it, what you found, and what you'd recommend to fix it.
  5. You'll present a 10–15 minute demo of your work to the class — be ready to explain your decisions, not just show a slide.
  6. Copying a report from a previous class or from the internet = automatic disqualification.




 Track 1 — GRC (Governance, Risk & Compliance)

You'll be given a fictional company. Your job is to think like a security manager, not a hacker: find the risks, map them to real frameworks, and tell
the company what to fix first.


  1. MedCore Clinics — Protecting Patient Data
  Your scenario: MedCore is a chain of clinics moving all patient records to a cloud-based system, with no security policy in place yet.
  What you'll do: Build a risk register for patient data, identify the realistic threats (insider misuse, ransomware, a careless cloud
  vendor), map your findings to a recognized framework (ISO 27001 or NIST CSF), and hand the clinic a prioritized list of what to fix first.
     Risk Analyst
     Compliance Officer
     Threat Modeler
     Control Owner / Auditor
     Policy Writer
     Team Lead / Presenter

    You must name a real framework you're using and build a scored risk matrix (likelihood × impact) covering at least 12 risks.



  2. NileTrade — Getting Fintech Investment-Ready
  Your scenario: NileTrade is a payments startup that needs to prove to investors it takes security seriously before it gets funded.
  What you'll do: Run a gap analysis against PCI-DSS, draw a data-flow diagram of how card data moves through the company,
  identify the biggest threats along that flow, and write a board-level risk report.
     PCI-DSS Gap Analyst
     Data-Flow Architect
     Threat Analyst
     Policy Author
     Third-Party Risk Reviewer
     Team Lead / Presenter

    Your data-flow diagram must be a real diagram, not a description. Classify at least 3 vendors by risk level.
3. Atlas Manufacturing — Securing a Smart Factory
Your scenario: Atlas just connected its old factory equipment (ICS/SCADA) to the corporate network so managers can monitor it
remotely.
What you'll do: Analyze the risk of mixing IT and factory-floor (OT) networks, identify threats unique to industrial equipment (old
protocols, unpatched control panels), and design a governance plan that both IT and factory managers can follow.
  OT Risk Analyst
  IT/OT Segmentation Reviewer
  Threat Modeler
  Business Impact Analyst
  Governance/Policy Lead
  Presenter

 Include a Business Impact Analysis estimating what downtime would cost the factory. Propose a network segmentation plan.



4. EduCloud — A University Moves to the Cloud
Your scenario: A university wants to move student records and research data to a public cloud, and leadership needs a clear yes-or-
no recommendation.
What you'll do: Assess the risk of the migration, review who is responsible for what between the university and the cloud provider,
evaluate the provider itself, and deliver a formal go/no-go recommendation.
  Cloud Risk Analyst
  Vendor Assessor
  Identity & Access Reviewer
  Compliance Checklist Owner
  Threat Analyst
  Presenter / Lead

 You must clearly state what the cloud provider secures versus what the university still has to secure, and justify your final recommendation.



5. PortLogix — Managing Third-Party Risk at a Seaport
Your scenario: PortLogix relies on dozens of outside vendors and contractors with remote access into its systems.
What you'll do: Build a vendor risk-management program: inventory the vendors, review their access levels, think through supply-
chain attack scenarios, and write a policy for what happens if a vendor gets breached.
  Supply-Chain Risk Analyst
  Vendor Access Reviewer
  Threat Modeler
  Business Continuity Planner
  Policy Writer
  Presenter

 Build a vendor risk-tiering table (Critical/High/Medium/Low) with your reasoning, and reference one real-world supply-chain breach as a case
 study.



6. SkyRoute Airlines — Third-Party App and Airport Systems Risk
Your scenario: SkyRoute just launched a new booking app built by an outside contractor and integrated it with internal airport check-
in systems.
What you'll do: Assess the risk introduced by the new app and its contractor, evaluate the integration points with internal systems,
and produce a risk acceptance report with sign-off recommendations for management.
  Application Risk Analyst
  Integration Risk Reviewer
  Threat Modeler
  Contractor/Vendor Assessor
  Policy Writer
  Presenter

 You must identify at least 3 integration points between the app and internal systems and rate the risk of each individually.
  7. GreenGrid Utility — Risk Assessment for Power Infrastructure
  Your scenario: GreenGrid manages regional power distribution and is under pressure to prove resilience against cyberattacks after a
  similar utility was hit elsewhere.
  What you'll do: Perform a critical infrastructure risk assessment, identify threats to availability (the utility's top priority), and design
  an incident-notification and regulatory-reporting policy.
     Critical Infrastructure Risk Analyst
     Availability/Resilience Reviewer
     Threat Modeler
     Regulatory/Reporting Policy Writer
     Business Continuity Planner
     Presenter

    Your risk assessment must rank availability threats as the top priority and justify why, given the utility's role.




 Track 2 — Penetration Testing

You'll be given a vulnerable target environment. Your job is to find real, exploitable vulnerabilities — not just run a scanner — and prove impact.


  8. Web App Pentest — "ShopEase"
  Your target: A deliberately vulnerable e-commerce web app provided in your lab.
  What you'll do: Test the app against the OWASP Top 10 (SQL injection, XSS, broken authentication, IDOR, etc.), chain vulnerabilities
  together to reach admin access or sensitive data, and write a professional report with CVSS scores for every finding.
     Recon Lead
     Web App Exploiter (Auth/Session)
     Web App Exploiter (Injection)
     Privilege Escalation Specialist
     Evidence/Documentation Lead
     Report Writer / Presenter

    You need at least 5 distinct vulnerability types, each manually verified and exploited — scanner output alone doesn't count.



  9. Internal Network Pentest — "Corplab"
  Your target: A simulated corporate network with a domain controller, file server, and workstations.
  What you'll do: Start from a single compromised workstation. Enumerate the network, harvest credentials, move laterally, and
  escalate all the way to Domain Admin.
     Network Enumeration Lead
     Credential Harvester
     Lateral Movement Specialist
     Privilege Escalation Specialist
     Persistence/Cleanup Lead
     Report Writer / Presenter

    Submit an attack-path diagram from your starting foothold to Domain Admin, and demonstrate at least one credential-based attack (pass-
    the-hash, Kerberoasting, etc.) with evidence.



  10. Wireless & IoT Pentest — "SmartHomeLab"
  Your target: A small office lab with Wi-Fi and several IoT devices (cameras, smart plugs, a router) left on weak or default settings.
  What you'll do: Attack the wireless network, find and exploit weaknesses in the IoT devices (default credentials, exposed services),
  and try to pivot from a compromised device into the wider network.
     Wireless Attack Lead
     IoT Device Analyst
     Rogue AP / MITM Specialist
     Pivoting Specialist
     Evidence Collector
     Report Writer / Presenter

    Document at least one successful wireless attack and one IoT compromise. Any pivot into the network must be diagrammed and backed by
    screenshots.
  11. Social Engineering & Phishing Assessment — "HR Test"
  Your target: A mock company's staff, played by another team or provided by your instructor.
  What you'll do: Design and run a phishing simulation with tracking, build a believable pretext for a social engineering scenario, and
  analyze why it worked (or didn't) — then recommend fixes.
    Phishing Campaign Designer
    Pretext/Scenario Writer
    Infrastructure Setup (tracking/landing page)
    Metrics & Analytics Lead
    Awareness Recommendations Lead
    Report Writer / Presenter

    Get written sign-off from your instructor before running anything — no real external targets, ever. Report your actual click-rate and
    credential-submission numbers, not estimates.



  12. API & Mobile Backend Pentest — "RideNow"
  Your target: A mock ride-hailing app's backend (REST/GraphQL API) with weak access controls and authentication.
  What you'll do: Map every API endpoint, then test for broken object-level authorization, excessive data exposure, weak tokens/JWTs,
  and privilege escalation between rider, driver, and admin roles.
    API Recon/Mapping Lead
    Auth/Token Exploiter
    BOLA/Access Control Specialist
    Fuzzing/Input Testing Lead
    Evidence Documenter
    Report Writer / Presenter

    Map the full API before you start attacking — no blind fuzzing as your only method. Demonstrate one full privilege escalation from a low-level
    account to an admin action.



  13. Cloud Environment Pentest — "CloudNest"
  Your target: A lab cloud environment (e.g., AWS/Azure sandbox) with misconfigured storage buckets, over-permissive IAM roles, and
  exposed credentials.
  What you'll do: Enumerate cloud resources, find misconfigurations (public buckets, weak IAM policies), escalate privileges within the
  cloud account, and demonstrate what an attacker could access or steal.
    Cloud Recon Lead
    IAM/Privilege Escalation Specialist
    Storage/Data Exposure Specialist
    Credential Hunter
    Evidence Documenter
    Report Writer / Presenter

    Only touch resources inside the lab cloud account you're given. Document the full privilege escalation path with proof of what you could
    ultimately access.



  14. Physical & Badge Security Assessment — "OfficeBreach"
  Your target: A simulated office environment (can be tabletop/staged) with badge access, unlocked workstations, and unattended
  documents.
  What you'll do: Assess physical security controls, attempt simulated tailgating/badge-cloning scenarios (as approved by your
  instructor), and identify how easily an attacker could gain a foothold through physical access.
    Physical Recon Lead
    Badge/Access Control Analyst
    Social Engineering Support
    Evidence Collector
    Risk-to-Digital Impact Analyst
    Report Writer / Presenter

    Nothing in this project happens on a real, non-sanctioned site — get instructor approval for the exact scenario first. Tie every physical
    weakness you find to a digital-security consequence.




 Track 3 — SOC / Attack & Defend (Red vs. Blue)

Your team of 6 splits into 3 attackers (Red) and 3 defenders (Blue). Red attacks a target environment while Blue watches, detects, and responds —
live.


  15. Live Ransomware Simulation — "CryptoWatch"
  The exercise: Red tries to deploy a simulated (harmless) ransomware payload across a small lab network. Blue has to catch it via
  SIEM/EDR alerts before it fully "encrypts" everything.
  Red's job: Get initial access, move laterally, trigger the payload. Blue's job: Monitor logs and alerts, detect the intrusion, contain the
  affected hosts, and write up a full incident timeline.
        Red: Initial Access Operator, Lateral Movement Operator, Payload/Execution Operator
        Blue: SOC Analyst/Detection Lead, Incident Responder/Containment Lead, Log & Timeline Analyst

        Blue doesn't get told Red's attack path in advance. Blue's report must show a full timeline comparing when the attack started versus when it
        was detected. Red logs every action taken for the after-action review.



  16. Web Server Compromise & Detection — "PublicPortal"
  The exercise: Red targets a public-facing web server running a vulnerable CMS to get a foothold and stay persistent. Blue watches
  web and network logs for signs of trouble.
  Red's job: Exploit the app, get a reverse shell, set up persistence. Blue's job: Catch the exploitation attempt, find the persistence
  mechanism, remove it, and document indicators of compromise.
        Red: Exploitation Lead, Shell/C2 Operator, Persistence Specialist
        Blue: Network Traffic Analyst, Host/Log Analyst, Incident Responder & IOC Writer

        Blue must produce a usable list of indicators of compromise (IPs, file hashes, persistence artifacts). Red has a fixed time window (e.g., 90
        minutes) to get in and stay in.



  17. Insider Threat & Data Exfiltration — "LeakWatch"
  The exercise: Red plays a malicious insider with valid low-level credentials trying to quietly steal sensitive data. Blue has to notice
  the unusual data movement.
  Red's job: Escalate privileges quietly and exfiltrate a marked file set without setting off obvious alarms. Blue's job: Baseline normal
  activity, catch the anomaly, and respond with account lockdown and a report.
        Red: Privilege Escalation Operator, Exfiltration Channel Specialist, Anti-Detection/Evasion Operator
        Blue: Baseline/Anomaly Analyst, DLP/Network Monitor, Incident Responder & Reporting Lead

        Red must actually exfiltrate the specific "flag" file your instructor gives you to prove success. Blue must name the exact alert or log entry
        that led to detection — no vague claims.



  18. Active Directory Attack & Defense — "DomainSiege"
  The exercise: Red attacks a small Active Directory environment using common techniques (Kerberoasting, password spraying, ACL
  abuse). Blue watches AD/Windows event logs and tries to stop Red before Domain Admin is reached.
  Red's job: Start with a low-privilege account and escalate to Domain Admin. Blue's job: Catch the suspicious
  authentication/escalation activity, respond, and harden the environment while the exercise is running.
        Red: Password Spray/Initial Access Operator, Kerberoasting/AD Enumeration Operator, Escalation/Domain Admin Operator
        Blue: AD/Event Log Analyst, SOC Alert Triage Lead, Hardening & Response Lead

        Blue can apply live fixes mid-exercise (disable an account, reset a password) but must log each action with a timestamp and reason. The
        round ends when Red reaches Domain Admin or Blue fully contains the attack — whichever happens first.



  19. DDoS & Availability Attack/Defense — "UptimeGuard"
  The exercise: Red launches a controlled, lab-only denial-of-service style attack against a target service. Blue has to keep the service
  up.
  Red's job: Degrade or take down the target using traffic-based or application-layer attack techniques. Blue's job: Monitor availability
  and traffic, spot the attack pattern, and mitigate it in real time (rate limiting, filtering, failover).
        Red: Traffic Generation Lead, Application-Layer Attack Specialist, Attack Timing/Coordination Lead
        Blue: Traffic/Availability Monitor, Mitigation Engineer, Incident Reporting Lead

        All traffic stays strictly inside your isolated lab range — never aimed at anything real or external. Blue is graded on the uptime percentage
        they manage to hold during the attack window.
  20. Phishing-to-Breach Full Chain — "InboxZero"
  The exercise: Red sends a simulated phishing email to a "victim" workstation in the lab to gain initial access, then tries to pivot
  deeper into the network. Blue monitors email gateway logs, endpoint alerts, and network traffic to catch the chain as early as possible.
  Red's job: Get a click, establish a foothold, and attempt at least one lateral move. Blue's job: Detect the phishing attempt (or its
  payload), isolate the affected host, and trace how far Red got before containment.
     Red: Phishing/Delivery Operator, Foothold/Payload Operator, Lateral Movement Operator
     Blue: Email/Gateway Analyst, Endpoint Detection Analyst, Incident Responder & Timeline Writer

    The phishing email only ever goes to the lab "victim" account — never to a real inbox. Blue's final report must state exactly where in the
    chain the attack was stopped (email, endpoint, or network stage).
